# TERT-gene-analysis-article
## TRANSCRIPT AND PROTEINISOFORM ANALYSIS OF TERT GENE

Telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) is a component of the larger enzyme Telomerase, which in addition to the TERT also consists of a RNA component. Together, they perform the function of the telomerase which is to preserve the telomeres of chromosomes by addition of the telomere repeats TTAGGG. This is important because it protects the DNA during its replicative cycle and prevents the replicative senescence that would ensue once the telomeres are fully degraded and the DNA exposed to damage. It is also implicated in tumorigenesis and maybe even more importantly, in aging. 
The TERT gene is located at chr5 p15.33 which is a location near the tip (telomere) of the chromosome. It is about 41, 900 base pairs(bp) long. The gene is made up of 16 exons of varying lengths, the largest being about 1354 bp long and the shortest about 62 bp long.  
In the ensemble release 2026-07, the TERT gene has 12 transcripts comprising of 5 protein coding transcripts, and 7 non-protein coding transcripts (which include transcripts with retained introns and those of nonsense-mediated-decay biotype). One transcript is annotated as “protein-coding-CDS-not-defined” which means that a coding sequence has not been found for that transcript, while the other four protein coding transcripts with defined coding sequences each have 15, 16 or 17 exons. 
Four known protein isoforms are produced from the TERT gene by alternative splicing. The canonical sequence chosen on Uniprot has 1132 amino acids, the other isoforms has lengths of 807, 1069, and 795 amino acids. 
The existence of various transcripts and protein isoforms of the TERT gene and its gene product has important implications. One of which is that the expression and function of the gene could potentially be controlled by alternative splicing and by factors which would modulate the alternative splicing, driving the preferential production of one or more transcripts and isoforms based on different signals.
